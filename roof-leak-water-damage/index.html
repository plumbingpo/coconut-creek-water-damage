Roof Leak Water Damage in Coconut Creek: What to Know

A roof leak rarely starts where the stain appears. A loose shingle, a failed flashing joint or an aging seal around a skylight lets rain in, and it travels across the roof deck and framing until it falls into the insulation. Often the attic has been damp for some time before any brown mark reaches the room below. We find the full wet area and dry it out.

Our trade is water damage restoration, and roofing is a separate one. Our job is the inside of the house: pulling out soaked insulation, drying the framing and drywall, treating for mold and putting the ceiling back together. If rain is still coming in, we can tarp the roof as a temporary cover, or work alongside the roofer you choose so the leak is fixed before we close anything up.

Do This First

Before We Arrive

  1. Shut Off Wet CircuitsWater coming through a ceiling fan, a light or close to an outlet means that circuit should be turned off at the panel, and the fixture should not be touched.
  2. Catch the DripsFurniture, electronics and rugs should be moved aside, with buckets placed wherever water is falling. Protect the floor with plastic sheeting or old towels.
  3. Drain a Bulging CeilingA ceiling that sags with trapped water can be drained: stay off to the side and make a small opening at its lowest spot, with a bucket underneath.
  4. Leave the Roof AloneClimbing on slick shingles or crawling around a dark attic is not worth the risk. Note from inside where the water is coming in and leave the rest to professionals.
  5. Document What You SeeTake photos and video of the ceiling, the attic and any damaged belongings before you move or throw anything away, then call us to start drying.
Insurance

What About Insurance?

Homeowners policies often cover interior water damage when a roof leak is sudden and accidental, such as wind tearing off shingles or a limb puncturing the roof. Leaks blamed on age, wear or skipped maintenance are usually denied, and the roof repair itself may be handled differently from the interior. Photos and moisture readings from us go to the adjuster, though only your policy decides coverage, and your agent can explain it.

Common Causes

What Causes It

  • Corroded or loose metal flashing where the roof meets a chimney, a vent or a wall.
  • Sun-cracked rubber collars where plumbing vent pipes come through the roof.
  • Skylight seals and curbs that dry out, crack and let rain run inside.
  • Missing or broken shingles, often after high wind, with bare underlayment beneath.
  • Water backing up at the eave and under the shingles because the gutters are blocked.
Why Speed Matters

What Happens if You Wait

Inside the Walls

Rain sometimes follows the framing into a wall, where the insulation and the hidden side of the drywall take it up. Often the one clue is paint that blisters or trim that swells at the floor.

Hidden Attic Mold

Damp framing, paper-faced drywall, warmth and darkness give mold what it needs. Growth can begin within 24 to 48 hours and spread across the underside of the decking.

Lights and Wiring

Recessed cans, ceiling fans and electrical boxes are low points where roof water collects and drips through. Wet connections can short or corrode, so the circuit should stay off until it is checked.

Insulation in the Attic

For days after a leak, matted insulation sits wet on top of the ceiling and no longer insulates. Fiberglass can sometimes be dried. Soaked cellulose usually has to be removed.
